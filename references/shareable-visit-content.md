# Turning a Visit into Shareable Content · 看展之后的可传播内容

Optional orientation for the moment after a visit, when the user wants to turn what they saw into something they can post — a caption set, a short note, or a sticker/表情包 idea.

This page is guidance only. **`museum-explorer` does not generate images, stickers, or posts, and does not publish anything.** When image production is actually needed, hand the user off to a dedicated image skill and stay out of the way.

## The one thing that matters: two different rights

A museum object and a photograph of that object are **separate works with separate rights**. Getting this wrong is the usual reason a well-meaning share goes bad.

1. **The object itself.** For modern/contemporary pieces, check the maker's death year. In China, copyright runs for the author's life plus 50 years (to the end of the 50th year). Once that expires, the object's design is in the public domain and may be freely redrawn.
   - Worked example: 曾龙升 (1900/1901–1964), whose 釉下加彩十八罗汉塑像 set is held by 景德镇中国陶瓷博物馆. Protection expired at the end of 2014, so the sculpture itself is public domain. **Its look may be redrawn — this does not license other people's photos of it.**
   - For older pieces the protection term is usually long expired, but the rule to check is the same, and provenance/repatriation questions are a separate matter to present fairly.
2. **The photograph.** A photo someone else took is that photographer's copyrighted work, regardless of who made the object. "The sculpture is public domain" is never a reason to reuse a stranger's photo.
3. **The venue's rules.** Photography, tripods, flash, live-streaming, and commercial use are all set by the venue, not by copyright law. Ask, or check the venue's own notice.
4. **The museum's own IP.** A museum's mascot, logo, or merchandise design can carry its own trademark/copyright even when the underlying artifact is public domain.
5. **Never imply endorsement.** Do not suggest an official collaboration, licence, or approval unless there is one in writing.

## Compliance ladder · 风险阶梯

| Use | Risk | Safe route |
|---|---|---|
| Private use, own reference | Low | Anything you personally shot, for your own eyes |
| Personal share (朋友圈 / 小红书 / 微博, non-commercial) | Low–moderate | **Your own photos**, venue photo rules respected, no third-party images |
| Illustrating a post with an original drawing | Low | Redraw it yourself / generate an original illustration of a public-domain object |
| Selling or listing on a commercial platform (e.g. 微信表情开放平台, 文创周边) | High | **Original artwork only.** Never build on someone else's photo. Verify venue and museum IP position first |

The practical rule: **if you did not shoot it, do not post it; if you want to sell it, draw it yourself.**

## A workflow that stays clean

1. **On site**: shoot your own photos. Photograph the label (名称 / 年代 / 作者, 展签 or 二维码) so facts stay accurate later.
2. **In the gallery**: keep it quick and courteous — some pieces have guarded, queued viewing (see the 景德镇 entry in `venue-starter-packs.md`), so a long photo session is both impractical and unpopular.
3. **After**: write down 1–3 facts you actually verified, plus your own reaction. Keep "verified fact" and "my interpretation" separate — the same discipline as Remember Mode.
4. **If an image is wanted**: generate an **original** illustration or sticker from those facts, rather than cropping someone's photo. This is where a dedicated image/sticker skill takes over.
5. **Captions**: prefer your own words. If the object is public domain, naming maker, date, and holding museum is both accurate and good practice.

## Why 景德镇's 沉思罗汉 is a natural fit

- The piece already **is** the joke: the public reads its expression as 「无语」, and the museum's own government profile acknowledges the 「无语菩萨」 meme as a visitor draw. You are not manufacturing a meme, you are joining an existing one.
- Demand is proven: it went viral from October 2023 (抖音热榜, a large jump in footfall), the museum moved the figure to a central position, and a viewing line with barriers became normal.
- Which is exactly why the **photo path is weak**: crowded, queued, guarded viewing makes a clean self-shot hard to get.
- So the honest recommendation here is the **original-illustration path**: redraw the pose and expression in your own style — legitimate, since the sculpture is public domain — and attach your own caption. Stronger output, no photo rights, no crowd.

## Handoff boundary

- This skill: plans, explains, remembers, and — on request — **advises on the compliant path**.
- A dedicated sticker/image skill: produces the actual artwork and can check compliance for platform listing.
- This skill never publishes, uploads, or posts on the user's behalf.
